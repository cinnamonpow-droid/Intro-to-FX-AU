MICRO-MODULE 1: Overview of Australian FX Market Regulation

There are three main regulators which oversee aspects of FX trading and the FX market in Australia. Click the cards below to learn about each one:

Tap cards to flip ↺
ASIC
Australia’s markets and financial services conduct regulator. Issues Australian Financial Services Licences (AFSL).
RBA
The central bank of Australia. Focuses on the stability of the financial system, with a particular connection to the FX market.
AUSTRAC
Australia’s financial intelligence agency. Responsible for anti-money laundering, counter-terrorism financing, and reporting.

MICRO-MODULE 2: Enforcement Outcomes

Following its investigations, ASIC has implemented a series of substantial enforcement outcomes relating to FX. All of the five major Australian banks have agreed to wide-ranging Enforceable Undertakings.

Tap cards to review key enforcement cases ↺
ASIC Enforceable Undertakings
Failings included: Disclosure of confidential client orders, trading inconsistent with orders, and attempts to unduly influence the market.
Insider Trading in FX
In 2015, two margin FX traders were sentenced to jail (up to 7 years) for trading on non-public ABS statistical information, netting $7 million.
AUSTRAC Reporting Failure
An FX business was fined $100k and its manager jailed for 10 months for avoiding standard significant cash transaction reports to AUSTRAC.

MICRO-MODULE 3: Contraventions

The Australian Corporations Act and the ASIC Act both have provisions which are relevant to conduct issues in the FX market. Contraventions can lead to administrative action and civil or criminal prosecution.

Tap cards to reveal prohibited behaviors ↺
Insider Trading
Includes trading on inside info, tipping others to trade, disclosing confidential client order info, and receiving/acting on confidential info.
Market Manipulation
Manipulating the price (particularly at key fixing times), false trading, market rigging, and trading with no change in beneficial ownership.
Misleading Conduct
Making false or misleading statements and inducing someone to deal based on those statements.

Knowledge Check

Contraventions of your obligations under your AFS Licence can lead to civil or criminal prosecution against the firm but not an individual.

MICRO-MODULE 4: Conduct and Behaviour

In May 2017, ASIC released Report 525, “Promoting better behaviour: Spot FX”. It details concerns and sets clear guidance on behavior expectations.

Tap cards to compare Concerns vs. Expectations ↺
ASIC Concerns
Lack of training, poor handling of confidential info, vague performance targets, and failing to detect or address misconduct.
ASIC Expectations
Supervisors model expected standards, foster an environment to challenge inappropriate practices, and impose visible sanctions for misconduct.
FX Global Code
Released in mid-2017, it is a key document setting out high-level regulatory expectations. All FX market individuals must understand it.

Knowledge Check

ASIC expects that supervisors should be held to account where their behaviour has not been adequate.

MICRO-MODULE 5: Rules and Regulations

All designated services in financial products are covered by AUSTRAC’S requirements, including making a market and dealing with clients in FX and derivatives.

Tap cards to learn about Key Classifications ↺
AUSTRAC Obligations
Implement a comprehensive AML/CTF Program, conduct customer due diligence, and report significant cash transactions (over $10k).
Retail vs. Wholesale
All clients are considered Retail (requiring extra protections) unless they explicitly pass the 'Wholesale Test'.
The Wholesale Test
Criteria include: Product price is $500k+, or client has certified net assets of at least $2.5m or income of $250k+ over the last two years.

Knowledge Check

If you deal with clients in FX, as long as you are enrolled with AUSTRAC, there is no need to implement an AML/CTF Program.
